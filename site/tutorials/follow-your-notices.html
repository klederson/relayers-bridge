AN EVERYDAY ROUTINE

A hypothetical shop has prepared its first path: new-order notices go to the team’s system. The person coordinating daily work wants to know whether information is arriving and who to call when something stops.

Start with three questions: did the notice appear, did it reach the destination and did the expected result happen? Each answer covers a different part of the path.

Before you start

  • A receiving path already prepared.
  • Access to the RELAYERS account used by the business.
  • Someone responsible for the destination tool.

A PATH YOU CAN UNDERSTAND

From a notice to a next step.

Follow one known occurrence from beginning to end. Receipt in RELAYERS, forwarding and the destination’s action are separate steps.

01

STEP 1

Sign in to the account your business uses.

Open the RELAYERS panel with the agreed access. If you use Desktop to receive on a computer, open that app too and check that it is connected.

Sign in to RELAYERS ↗Create an account ↗
Use the account that organizes the path you want to follow.
02

STEP 2

Look for a notice you can identify.

Choose a recent order or occurrence the team recognizes. Find its incoming notice in RELAYERS. Compare the time and information to recognize the right occurrence.

A known example is more useful than searching a whole list without knowing what you need.
03

STEP 3

Check forwarding to the destination.

Open that notice’s details and follow its delivery attempts. Look for whether the destination received it, an attempt is still underway or a failure needs attention.

The history helps describe what happened. It does not replace checking the action inside the destination tool.
04

STEP 4

Check the team’s tool too.

If the agreement was to create an order-picking task, ask the team to find it. If the destination received the notice but the task did not appear, its owner needs to examine what happened next.

This lets you explain which step differed from the expected result.
05

STEP 5

Ask for help with a simple description.

Say which notice you expected, when it happened, how far you could follow it and which result was missing. For example: “The test order appeared in RELAYERS, but I could not find the task in our system.”

You do not need to send a password to explain a problem. The responsible person can investigate using their own access.
06

STEP 6

Agree before making another attempt.

If someone suggests sending a notice again, first check whether the destination received it or the action already happened. Agree who makes another attempt and how to check its result.

Another attempt can repeat an action in the receiving system. Consider what has already happened in that case.

QUESTIONS WHEN YOU ARE STARTING

You can ask.

Will notices reach my computer with Desktop closed?

Local receipt depends on Desktop or another receiving connection being available. Check the history and agree how to resume after an interruption.

Can I pause a path?

RELAYERS supports pausing forwarding rules. Check which rule is selected and the intended effect before changing the routine.

Does RELAYERS perform the task inside my system?

RELAYERS forwards the notice. The destination tool is responsible for using the information in the agreed task or action.

A CLEAR FIRST RESULT

Less guessing. A description that helps people resolve the issue.

You can show what arrived, what was forwarded and where the team should look next. That clarity helps both the people following daily work and the people maintaining the tools.

  • I found a known occurrence.
  • I checked its forwarding.
  • The team checked the destination result.
  • Someone is responsible for failures.
  • Another attempt happens only after these checks.
