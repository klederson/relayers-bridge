START FROM ZERO

Imagine your business receives notices from several tools: an order was placed, a payment was confirmed, someone completed a form. Each piece of news arrives in a different way. RELAYERS helps receive these notices and forward them to the place you choose so work can continue.

Think of a business mailroom: a notice arrives, the agreed path is checked and the information goes to its destination. You decide what should happen. Connecting the tools initially may need help from the person who looks after them; understanding the process and following the result does not require programming.

Before you start

  • A RELAYERS account.
  • A tool with a way to share its notices.
  • A destination that can receive those notices.
  • One simple situation and someone to help connect it if needed.

A PATH YOU CAN UNDERSTAND

From a notice to a next step.

For example: a shop records a new order, RELAYERS forwards the notice and the team’s chosen system can receive it. The notice follows this path after the connection has been prepared.

01

STEP 1

Start with the problem you want to solve.

Use one sentence: “When an order arrives, I want the delivery team to know.” You have already defined the important parts: what happens, who needs to know and where the information should appear.

Choose one situation. A small first case is easier to follow than connecting your whole business at once.
02

STEP 2

Create an account and sign in.

Use “Create an account” if this is your first visit. Enter your name, email and password on the RELAYERS page. Then use “Sign in to RELAYERS” to open your panel. If you already have an account, sign in directly.

Sign in to RELAYERS ↗Create an account ↗
Your account is where you organize incoming notices and follow what happened. Use the access your business has agreed on for this work.
03

STEP 3

Choose where the notice comes from.

It could be a shop, payment system or form that offers a way to notify other tools. Pick one specific occurrence, such as “order placed”.

RELAYERS receives what the source is prepared to share. Signing in does not automatically connect all your systems.
04

STEP 4

Choose where the notice should arrive.

It might be a system your team uses or an application running on the business computer. Name the destination and what people expect to find there, such as a new order-picking task.

Receiving the notice and creating a task are different jobs. The chosen system must be prepared to turn the notice into the intended action.
05

STEP 5

Prepare the path between both sides.

In RELAYERS, organize an entry for incoming notices and a path to the destination. The source tool uses the receiving address supplied by RELAYERS. If these fields are unfamiliar, ask the person who looks after the tools to connect them with you.

A useful request is: “I want this notice from this tool to reach this system.” You do not need to know installation details to define the result.
06

STEP 6

If the destination is on your computer, meet Desktop.

RELAYERS Desktop brings notices to a tool running on your computer. Download it from the official page, open it and sign in to the same account. “Forwarding” is where you choose where notices should arrive.

The app needs to stay connected for local receipt. For a destination available on the internet, agree with its owner how forwarding should reach it.

07

STEP 7

Try an example you can recognize.

Create a test order using demonstration details agreed with your team. Check that the notice appears in RELAYERS and then at the destination. Compare the result with your sentence from the first step.

The aim is to find that notice in the right place. Seeing it only in RELAYERS does not confirm the destination completed its part.
08

STEP 8

Follow and adjust the routine.

Look at incoming notices and forwarding attempts in the panel or app. Agree who follows the flow and what to do when a notice needs attention. To stop a path, pause its forwarding rule.

Before requesting another attempt, check whether the destination already received the notice. This helps avoid repeating a task or action.

QUESTIONS WHEN YOU ARE STARTING

You can ask.

Do I need to program?

Not to understand the process, define the result and follow everyday work. Connecting the tools depends on what each offers and may need someone experienced with their settings.

Is it an app for chatting with customers?

RELAYERS primarily receives and forwards notices between tools. A customer conversation or service screen belongs to the system you choose for that work.

What is an entry?

It is the agreed place where a tool delivers notices to RELAYERS. Think of a mailbox with its own address.

What is a destination?

It is the tool or application that should receive the notice afterward. It is where the information supports your team’s work.

What is a forwarding rule?

It is the agreement that says where a notice should go. Different needs can use different paths.

Does a received notice mean everything is done?

It confirms one part of the path. Also check forwarding to the destination and the result your team expected there.

A CLEAR FIRST RESULT

You know what arrives, where it goes and who follows it.

That is a useful first result: a simple path your team understands and has checked with an example. Once it works, you can consider a second need more clearly.

  • I can describe the notice that starts the process.
  • I know its source tool and destination.
  • I have an account and know where to check incoming notices.
  • The connection was prepared with the responsible person.
  • A test showed the notice at the expected destination.
