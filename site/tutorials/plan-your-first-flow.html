PREPARE WITH YOUR TEAM

A small business wants to connect its tools, but the discussion starts with unfamiliar terms. The person who knows the business understands what the team needs; they need to turn it into a clear request.

Begin with the routine: what happens, who needs to know, where information should arrive and how you will recognize success. RELAYERS can organize forwarding when the tools involved offer the necessary connection.

Before you start

  • One concrete team need.
  • The names of the tools involved.
  • Someone familiar with those tools’ access and settings.

A PATH YOU CAN UNDERSTAND

From a notice to a next step.

A clear request connects a business need to a result the team can check. Setup follows that agreement.

01

STEP 1

Write down what happens first.

Use a familiar sentence: “Someone completes our quote form” or “An order is confirmed.” Choose one occurrence and the tool where it happens.

“Automate everything” is hard to check. A defined occurrence gives your team a starting point.
02

STEP 2

Say where information should appear.

Name the tool your team uses and which information would be useful there. For example: “The confirmed order should reach the system that organizes deliveries.”

The destination must have a way to receive the notice. The person looking after the tools checks that possibility.
03

STEP 3

Describe what should happen afterward.

Agree whether the result is simply receiving information or also performing an action at the destination, such as creating a task. Make clear who prepares that action and who follows exceptions.

Forwarding the notice and acting on it are different responsibilities. Include both in the request.
04

STEP 4

Decide who takes part.

Choose who approves the business result, who prepares the connection and who follows daily use. Use the RELAYERS account and access agreed by your business.

A name for each responsibility helps people know whom to ask.
05

STEP 5

Agree on a test example.

Choose a fictional order or form everyone can recognize. Write where it should appear and what you will check at the destination. Ask the responsible person to explain how to stop the path if needed.

The test should show the team’s need being met as well as a notice being received.
06

STEP 6

Leave a simple written agreement.

Write: “When ___ happens in ___, we want ___ to arrive in ___. We will check whether ___ happened. ___ prepares it and ___ follows it.”

Use this sentence in a discussion, meeting or tracking task. Fill it with your business’s actual situation.

QUESTIONS WHEN YOU ARE STARTING

You can ask.

Do I need to choose installation details?

Begin with the need and expected result. The person who looks after the tools helps select and prepare the appropriate path.

How do I know whether the idea is possible?

Ask the responsible person to check whether the source can share its notice and the destination can receive it and perform the desired action.

When should we add another situation?

After checking the first example and agreeing who follows its use. Your team then knows the path before expanding the routine.

A CLEAR FIRST RESULT

A request your team can understand and check.

You leave the discussion with a defined need, named responsibilities and an agreed example of success. That gives the work of connecting tools a direction.

  • We defined a starting occurrence.
  • We know the source and destination.
  • We agreed which action belongs to the destination.
  • Someone prepares the path and someone follows it.
  • We know which example will show it worked.
